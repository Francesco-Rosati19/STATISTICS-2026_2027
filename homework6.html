1. Theoretical Foundations of Statistical Independence

A. Independence in Probability

Two events A and B are statistically independent if knowing that one occurred does not alter the probability of the other:

P(A ∩ B) = P(A) · P(B)
P(A | B) = P(A)   (for P(B) > 0)

For two discrete random variables X and F, independence requires the joint distribution to factorize into the product of marginals across all pairs (xᵢ, fⱼ).

B. Independence in Statistics (Sample)

Given N bivariate observations (X, F) with cell counts Oᵢⱼ, row totals Rᵢ, and column totals Cⱼ, expected frequencies under H₀ (independence) are:

Eᵢⱼ = N · (Rᵢ / N) · (Cⱼ / N) = (Rᵢ · Cⱼ) / N
f(F = fⱼ | X = xᵢ) = Oᵢⱼ / Rᵢ

Because finite samples exhibit random sampling noise, we test H₀: P(X, F) = P(X)P(F) using Pearson's Chi-square statistic.

C. Pearson's Chi-Square Statistic & Cramér's V

χ² = ∑ᵢ ∑ⱼ [ (Oᵢⱼ − Eᵢⱼ)² / Eᵢⱼ ]     with     df = (r − 1)(c − 1)
p-value = P(χ²_df ≥ χ²_obs) = Q(df / 2, χ²_obs / 2)   |   Cramér's V = √[ χ² / (N · min(r − 1, c − 1)) ]

3. Conclusions: Statistical Association vs. Causality

1. Interpreting the Chi-Square Result

• When p-value < α (e.g., 0.05): The observed discrepancy between Oᵢⱼ and Eᵢⱼ is too large to be explained by random sampling variation alone. We reject H₀ and conclude that X and F are statistically dependent.

• When p-value ≥ α: The data are compatible with the independence model Eᵢⱼ. Note that failing to reject H₀ does not mathematically prove exact independence, especially in small samples.

2. Association Does Not Imply Causation

The χ² test is completely symmetric in X and F, whereas causality is directional. Detecting a statistical association only proves that the joint distribution does not factorize; it can arise from:

  • Confounding Variables (Z): A hidden third factor (e.g., user seniority or department) influences both training completion (X) and phishing clicks (F).
  • Reverse Causality: Experiencing a security incident (F) might cause users to take training (X).
  • Selection Bias & Simpson's Paradox: Aggregating heterogeneous groups can create spurious associations or reverse real trends. Establishing causality requires Randomized Controlled Trials (RCTs).
