In-Depth Theoretical & Architectural Analysis

1. Comparative Matrix: Caesar Cipher vs. EC-ElGamal Scheme

Cryptographic Property Caesar Cipher Toy EC-ElGamal Scheme
Key Paradigm Symmetric key (single shared secret shift K ∈ Z₂₆). Asymmetric key pair (Private k, Public Q = kG, Ephemeral r).
Encryption Determinism Deterministic: Identical letters always yield identical ciphertext characters. Probabilistic: Fresh random r per block yields distinct ciphertexts for identical messages.
Key Space Cardinality Trivial: |K| = 26 possible shifts (25 non-trivial). Toy Model: |K| = 18 points; Real secp256k1: |K| ≈ 2²⁵⁶.
Language Statistics Preservation Preserved: Strictly preserves letter histograms and monographic distances. Destroyed: Scrambles point coordinates across finite field, masking letter frequencies.
Ciphertext Expansion Rate None (1:1 ratio, 1 byte in → 1 byte out). High (2 uncompressed points per byte ≈ 66× data expansion).

2. Evaluation of Statistical Cryptanalysis Attack Rules

1. Most Frequent Letter (E-Pivot)

Assumes the single most common letter in the ciphertext corresponds to 'E' (12.7% frequency in English).

Limitation: Highly brittle on short texts. If 'T', 'A', or 'O' appears more often in a small sample, the entire attack fails (20% accuracy at 10 letters).

2. Chi-Squared (χ²) Goodness-of-Fit

Evaluates all 26 observed character counts against standard English probability distributions:

χ²(K) = Σ [ (O_c - E_c)² / E_c ]

Strength: Leverages the full alphabet histogram. Reaches >95% accuracy once text exceeds 30 characters.

3. Dictionary Word Matching

Decrypts with candidate shift K and counts matches against top English words (e.g., "THE", "AND", "IS").

Limitation: Completely fails if spaces/punctuation are stripped (0% accuracy without spaces).

4. Trigram N-Gram Log-Likelihood

Computes log-probabilities over 3-letter sliding windows: Σ log P(x_j x_{j+1} x_{j+2}).

Strength: Highest accuracy on extremely short ciphertexts (>98% accuracy at 10 letters). Immune to missing spaces.

3. Attacking Toy ECC & Structural Weaknesses Beyond Key Space

Discrete Logarithm Algorithms

  • Exhaustive Search O(N): Computes 1G, 2G, 3G... until matching Q. Trivial on toy bounds (|K| ≤ 18).
  • Baby-Step Giant-Step (BSGS, O(√N)): Decomposes k = i · m + j where m = ⌈√N⌉. Precomputes jG table, reducing operations to ≈ √N.
  • Pollard's Rho / Kangaroo (O(√N)): Memoryless randomized walk algorithm. Reduces search space square root.
  • Infeasibility on Real secp256k1: For a 256-bit order n ≈ 2²⁵⁶, O(√n) = 2¹²⁸ operations. Even at 10¹² ops/sec, computation requires over 10¹⁸ years.

Architectural Flaws of Toy Scheme

  • Reusing Ephemeral r (Fatal Flaw): If r is reused across blocks, S = rQ remains constant. The scheme degrades into a monoalphabetic substitution cipher on point bytes, allowing full Caesar-style frequency analysis!
  • Ciphertext Malleability: Without an authentication tag (AEAD / HMAC), an attacker can compute C₂' = C₂ + G, predictably altering the decrypted payload without detection.
  • Lack of Hybrid Encryption: Pure ECC point encryption causes 66× storage expansion. Production systems use ECIES (ECC for key agreement + AES-GCM for data).
