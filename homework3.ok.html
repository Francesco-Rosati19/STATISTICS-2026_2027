In-Depth Theoretical Analysis & Statistical Interpretations

1. Stochastic Penetration Models

The security of the system infrastructure is evaluated through a discrete-time stochastic process. The increments Xᵢ represent the penetration score gained at each system step.

Bernoulli Model

Asymmetric model where an attacker either succeeds (+1) or fails (0).

  • Xᵢ ∈ {0, 1}
  • E[X] = p
  • Var(X) = p(1-p)

Rademacher Model

Symmetric zero-mean model representing balanced forward and backward steps.

  • Xᵢ ∈ {-1, +1}
  • E[X] = 0
  • Var(X) = 1

2. Law of Large Numbers (LLN) & Average Score

The Weak Law of Large Numbers dictates that the sample average converges in probability toward the expected value. Looking at the "Average Score" trajectories (Sₙ/n), they initially display large sample fluctuations, but as n grows, all trajectories compress tightly around the theoretical expected value line:

lim (n→∞) P(|Sₙ/n - E[X]| > ε) = 0

3. Behavior of the Variance Under Normalization

Normalizing the total sum Sₙ alters how the variance behaves across time:

Quantity Mathematical Form Variance Scaling
Total Score Sₙ Var(Sₙ) = n · σ² (Diverges Linearly)
Average Score Sₙ / n Var(Sₙ/n) = σ² / n (Converges to 0)
Normalized Score Sₙ / √n Var(Sₙ/√n) = σ² (Remains Constant)

4. Central Limit Theorem (CLT)

Because normalizing by √n stabilizes the variance, the distribution of Sₙ / √n converges in distribution to a Gaussian curve. In the Rademacher model (E[X]=0, Var(X)=1), it converges directly to the Standard Normal Distribution N(0, 1).

For the Bernoulli model, standardizing to a pure N(0, 1) requires centering by subtracting the mean: (Sₙ - np) / √(np(1-p)) → N(0, 1). When plotted directly as Sₙ / √n, the Gaussian curve shifts horizontally over time with mean p√n.

5. Interpreting Systems (n) and Attackers (m)

Systems (n): The Time Horizon

Represents the sequence length of system steps. As n → ∞, asymptotic limit theorems take control. The average score concentrates, and the standardized score distribution smooths out into a continuous Gaussian bell curve.

Attackers (m): Sample Resolution

Dictates the number of independent random walk trajectories simulated. Increasing m does not change the theoretical limits, but it increases the empirical resolution, making the density histogram fit the theoretical Gaussian curve with higher precision.
